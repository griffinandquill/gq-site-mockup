Subjects

U.S. History

Colonial arguments through modern policy, with the source beside the textbook story.

Government

How rules get made, who holds power, and how to read a founding text.

Economics

Scarcity, markets, and the choices a household or a town actually has.

AP Human Geography

Why people move, where they settle, and how a map argues. Often called APHG.

World History, study skills, and test prep sit inside these courses. They are not a separate brand. Study skills means notes, reading a prompt, and turning a source into a paragraph. Test prep explains why an answer works.

Formats

1:1

One student and one goal for the day. The student does the talking. Online first, for families, homeschoolers, and lifelong learners.

Small group

A few students stuck on the same course. The group stays small enough that every student speaks.

How tutoring starts

  1. 1

    Free consult

    We ask about the course, the sticking point, and the calendar. There is no charge, and it does not lock you into a plan.

  2. 2

    A plan

    You get a short written plan: the skills to work on, how often to meet, and what to practice between sessions.

  3. 3

    Sessions

    We meet online. Each session has one goal, time to try it, and a note you can reread later.

Questions

Is tutoring online?

Yes. Tutoring is online first. You meet by video. If you need a different setup, say so on the consult form. We will tell you if we can do it.

What subjects do you cover?

U.S. History, Government, Economics, and AP Human Geography. World History, study skills, and test prep can be the focus of a session when they belong to the course the student is in.

Who is tutoring for?

Parents, homeschoolers, and students, including adults who want to study for themselves. If you are a student, use a parent or guardian email on the form.

How does a new student start?

Three steps. A free consult, then a written plan, then sessions. The consult does not lock you into a paid plan.

Do you tutor small groups?

Yes. Small groups sit alongside 1:1 tutoring. A group stays small enough that every student speaks. The example pricing card shows a sample group rate, not a final price.

How much does tutoring cost?

Real rates are not set. The pricing section shows example tiers so you can see the layout. Each one is marked as an example. Do not budget from those numbers.
