Tutoring

One-to-one online tutoring.

One-to-one online tutoring is a flat $50 per hour. Sessions teach, grow, and maintain the academic skills a student needs to strengthen understanding in the courses they are taking.

Book with us

Book a free 15-minute consult on this page. This site does not take payment.

Subjects

Tutoring builds academic skills in whatever course a student is taking. Ryan Mayer currently teaches these courses:

U.S. History

U.S. History and the Constitution (USHC): reading a source and explaining a claim, from colonial arguments through modern policy.

Government

How rules get made, who holds power, and how to read a founding text.

Economics

Scarcity, markets, and the choices a household or a town actually has.

AP Human Geography

Why people move, where they settle, and how a map argues. Often called APHG.

A session can focus on the course a student is taking, including World History, study skills, and test prep when they belong to that course. Study skills means notes, reading a prompt, and turning a source into a paragraph. Test prep explains why an answer works.

How tutoring starts

  1. 1

    Skills check

    The first session starts with a quick skills check: the course, the sticking point, and what the student can already do.

  2. 2

    A plan

    You get a short written plan: the skills to work on, how often to meet, and what to practice between sessions.

  3. 3

    Sessions

    We meet online. Each session has one goal, time to try it, and a note you can reread later.

Rate and availability

One-to-one online tutoring is $50 per hour. The rate is flat. Sessions are available on weekday evenings and weekend afternoons. Book on this page first. This site does not take payment.

Questions

Is tutoring online?

Yes. Tutoring is online first. You meet by video. If you need a different setup, say so when you book. We will tell you if we can do it.

What subjects do you cover?

Sessions build academic skills in the subject a student is studying. Ryan Mayer teaches U.S. History, Government, Economics, and AP Human Geography. World History, study skills, and test prep can be the focus of a session when they belong to that course.

Who is tutoring for?

Parents, homeschool families, and students, including adults who want to study for themselves. A student should book through a parent or guardian.

How does a new student start?

Three steps. A skills check, then a written plan, then sessions. The first session starts with a quick skills check.
