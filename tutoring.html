Tutoring

One student, one plan, online.

1:1 and small-group tutoring in history and social studies, plus study skills and test prep. You start with a free consult. Nothing is billed from this mockup.

Book a free consult Example pricing

What is offered

1:1

One student and one goal for the day. The student does the talking. Online first, for families, homeschoolers, and lifelong learners.

Small group

A small group, kept small enough that every student speaks. Useful when a few students are stuck on the same course.

Study skills and test prep are part of tutoring, not a separate brand. They stay tied to the history or social studies course in front of the student.

Subjects

U.S. History

Colonial arguments through modern policy, with sources beside the textbook story.

World History

Trade, empire, revolution, and the people who lived through them.

Government

How rules get made, who holds power, and how to read a founding text.

Economics

Scarcity, markets, and the choices a household or a town actually has.

Human Geography

Why people move, where they settle, and how maps argue.

Study skills

Notes, reading a prompt, and turning a source into a paragraph.

Test prep

Practice that explains why an answer works, tied to the course the student is in.

How tutoring starts

  1. 1

    Free consult

    We ask about the course, the sticking point, and the calendar. There is no charge, and it does not lock you into a plan.

  2. 2

    A plan

    You get a short written plan: the skills to work on, how often to meet, and what to practice between sessions.

  3. 3

    Sessions

    We meet online. Each session has one goal, time to try it, and a note you can reread later.

Questions

Is tutoring online?

Yes. Tutoring is online first. You meet by video. If you need a different setup, say so on the consult form. We will tell you if we can do it.

What subjects do you cover?

U.S. History, World History, Government, Economics, and Human Geography. Sessions can also focus on study skills and test prep for those courses.

Who is tutoring for?

Parents, homeschoolers, and students, including adults who want to study history for themselves. If you are a student, use a parent or guardian email on the form.

How does a new student start?

Three steps. A free consult, then a written plan, then sessions. The consult does not lock you into a paid plan.

Do you tutor small groups?

Yes. Small groups sit alongside 1:1 tutoring. A group stays small enough that every student speaks. The example pricing card shows a sample group rate, not a final price.

How much does tutoring cost?

Real rates are not set. The Tutoring page shows example tiers so you can see the layout. Each one is marked as an example. Do not budget from those numbers.
